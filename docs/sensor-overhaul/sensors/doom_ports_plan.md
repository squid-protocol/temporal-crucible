# DOOM ports as a proving ground for the repair radius (plan, 2026-11-18)

**Status: plan; nothing registered or run.** Data: the 44 pinned ports of `doom-dna` (`/srv/storage_16tb/projects/doom-dna`, `manifest/manifest.csv`), now cloned with **full history** under `/srv/storage_16tb/projects/temporal-crucible-pool/doom-ports` (outside every repository; third-party code is never committed, measurements only). Inventory: `sensors/doom_inventory_output.txt`, `tools/sensor/doom_inventory.py`.

## 1. What exists
* **Function identity across ports, name-blind:** `doom-dna` (seed-and-extend, `followups/seed-and-extend`, standalone `structural-fingerprint-scanner`): FGW reciprocal-best-hit seeds, `delta` calibration and 3-hop call-graph extension, zero names read. Name agreement of the recovered matches against `linuxdoom` (its own scorecard, `FINDING.md`): C ports **80 to 100%** (mood 95%, doomgeneric 86%, sdl-doom 84%, chocolate-doom 67%, fastdoom 55%), **Go gore 77%**, doom3-bfg 40%, **Java mochadoom 18%, C# managed-doom 16%**, Dart 9%, Scheme 5% (their subsystem placement is better: 33 to 65%); from-scratch rewrites and unrelated C 0 to 1% (the null). Also `canonical_functions.csv` (168 canonical functions and the name each port uses) in `gitgalaxy/doom-study`.
* **Within-repo identity through history:** the function ledger (`structural_fingerprint.ledger.build_ledger`: one lineage id per function across snapshots, following renames and moves).
* **Call graphs and scans:** `gitgalaxy/doom-study/scans_r2` (39 ports, round 2) and doom-dna's scans.

## 3. Experiments, ranked by what the data supports
| # | question | design | history needed | power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Is the call graph right, per language?** A natural oracle. | Take `linuxdoom`'s call edges among functions matched 1-to-1 to a port (seed-and-extend matches, plus the canonical names where both exist); check whether the engine's graph of the port has the corresponding edge; recall and precision by language, port fidelity ("faithful" in the manifest) as the control. | none (scans exist) | all 44 ports; faithful ports give the cleanest reference |
| **B** | **Same neighbourhood, independent lineages: is the repair radius a property of the structure or of the people?** | For inherited functions (matched to `linuxdoom`), compute each lineage's repair radius with blame-based causes (calibrated on C: radius within 1 to 14% of trailers); compare across lineages and test whether neighbourhoods repaired together in one lineage are repaired together in the others. | yes (C and C++) | about 100,000 commits; thousands of pairs per lineage |
| **C** | **Convergent repair.** | For an inherited function repaired in port X, which neighbours were repaired with it; do **independent** lineages repair the same neighbours more often than chance (permutation null: random functions of the same size and file role); inherited fixes (shared commits or identical diffs) excluded. | yes | depends on overlap; the most informative if it works |
| **D** | **Does a sibling's history beat the local graph?** (the cross-port prior for "where to look first") | Predict where lineage X's repairs land from (a) X's own graph (the validated ranking), (b) the neighbours repaired in the other lineages, (c) both; held-out by lineage. | yes | lineages with at least 500 repair pairs |
| **E** | **Added and deleted inherited functions.** | Replicate the added and deleted function result where lineages drop or add inherited functions. | yes | thin; descriptive |
| **F** | **Known-bug case studies.** | Vanilla bugs that several ports fixed (documented in the community): compare the functions each fix touched. | yes | a handful; illustrative |
Not feasible as designed: a per-port repair radius in Java, C#, Go, Rust, Kotlin, Dart, Crystal and Scheme (histories of 20 to 830 commits), and any claim that rests on `helion`, `room4doom` or `rust-doom` being "the same neighbourhood" (they are from-scratch rewrites with 0% DNA).

## 4. Method notes
* **Function IDs through time:** align each port to `linuxdoom` at its pinned snapshot with the name-blind matcher, then carry the id backward through the port's history with the lineage ledger (quarterly snapshots; `turn_v2` scan harness). Names are used only as a check.
* **Independence:** families (above) are one lineage each; cross-lineage tests use one representative per family or a family-level random effect.
* **Cause labels:** none of these projects write `Fixes:` lines; blame-based causes (SZZ style) with the calibration already done on DPDK and curl; fix commits by subject words.
* **Pre-registration:** hypotheses written down before every run (as for every study in this log); the preregistration of record for the DOOM study is `doom-dna/preregistration/EXPERIMENTS.md` (DOOM-E1 to E6 exist); new experiments here would be DOOM-R1... and should be registered there before any scan or measurement.
* **Licences and data:** measurements only; clones live outside any repository; no source text is quoted.

## 5. Revision after reading the matcher (2026-11-18)
**Experiment A (call-graph oracle) is withdrawn as designed.** The seed-and-extend matcher extends its matches **along the port's own call graph** (`pv.edges`, and the hop matrix in the seeding), so matches from it cannot serve as an oracle for that graph: agreement would be partly built in. A name-based oracle would mostly repeat `callgraph_audit.py`. A graph-free identity (descriptors only, no edges) would be needed; not built.
**Name preservation** (inherited names, 765 distinct normalised function names of 3+ lines in `linuxdoom`, found in each port's scan; `gitgalaxy/doom-study/scans_r2`): puredoom 91%, doom3-bfg 91%, chocolate-doom and crispy-doom 87%, sdl-doom 87%, doomgeneric 86%, doom-ascii 86%, doom-wasm 85%, prboom-plus 79%, inter-doom 75%, dsda-doom 71%, nugget-doom 70%, fastdoom 70%, doomretro 68%, odamex 66%, eternity 66%, woof 50%, doom64ex 44%, srb2 42%; Java mochadoom 25%; C# managed-doom 1%, Go gore 0%, rust-doom 0%, helion 10%, room4doom 15%, gzdoom 20%, doomsday 28%. **So name identity works for the C and C++ family and not for the translations.**
**First experiment run: B, in C lineages only.** Lineage representatives (shared history counted once; independent lineages): **crispy-doom** (includes chocolate-doom's history), **dsda-doom** (includes prboom-plus), **nugget-doom** (includes woof), **doomretro**, **fastdoom**, **inter-doom**; about 47,000 commits. Quarterly scans from each project's first commit (turn_v2 harness), blame-based causes (calibrated on C: DPDK and curl), changed functions by tree-sitter in both versions. **Inherited function** = its normalised name occurs in `linuxdoom`.

## 6. DOOM-R1: repair around inherited functions in independent lineages (hypotheses fixed 2026-11-18 before any scan or measurement; to be registered in `doom-dna/preregistration` on the owner's word)
- **DB1 (replication).** The radius within 2 calls (repaired functions outside the changed set against same-file functions of similar size) is at least 1.3 with a lower bound above 1.1 in each of the six lineages with at least 100 usable pairs.
- **DB2 (structure, not people: where the radius sits).** Restricted to **inherited** functions on both sides (cause and repair), the radius is within 25% of the all-functions radius in each lineage (the inherited neighbourhood behaves like the rest).
- **DB3 (structure, not people: which functions).** Over inherited functions present in at least four lineages, the **number of times a function is repaired** (as a repaired function in a pair) correlates between lineages: mean pairwise Spearman correlation at least 0.20, and at least 0.10 after partialling out log size and call fan-in (computed in the linuxdoom graph).
- **DB4 (structure, not people: which neighbours).** For inherited cause-repair function pairs within 2 calls in the linuxdoom graph, a pair observed in one lineage is observed in another more often than a permutation null (repaired function replaced by a random inherited function of the same size decile and the same file prefix in the same lineage): observed overlap at least twice the null mean, pooled over lineage pairs. **Untestable if fewer than 30 such pairs exist in each of at least three lineages.**
- **DB5 (independence).** The shared-history families do not manufacture DB3 and DB4: the same statistics computed between crispy-doom and chocolate-doom (shared history) are at least twice those between independent lineages (a positive control: shared fixes are the same commits).
**Would surprise (the radius is about people, not structure):** DB3 below 0.05 after partialling out size and fan-in, **and** DB4 overlap at or below the null.
**Caveats fixed in advance.** Blame labels; C only (C++ and translations later); name identity (65% to 87% of names survive); lineages differ in age, size and activity, so a lineage-level variance component is reported next to every pooled figure; the radius has been shown elsewhere to depend on change size (small changes 2 to 3, large 1.2 to 1.3), so lineage comparisons are also shown by size band.

## 7. DOOM-R1 results (2026-11-18; `doom_r1_output.txt`, `tools/sensor/doom_r1.py`; blame-based causes, up to 3,000 fix-like commits per lineage; C lineages with full histories, scanned quarterly)
Radius within 2 calls (repaired functions outside the changed set against same-file functions of similar size): **all functions / inherited functions only** (pairs outside the changed set in brackets): crispy-doom **1.66** [1.48, 1.87] (308) / 1.13 [0.75, 1.59] (89); dsda-doom **1.66** [1.52, 1.84] (390) / 1.54 [1.10, 2.24] (87); nugget-doom **1.50** [1.36, 1.67] (233) / 1.54 [1.26, 1.87] (79); doomretro **1.82** [1.66, 2.00] (538) / 1.49 [1.25, 1.76] (199); fastdoom 1.37 [1.13, 1.65] (85) / 1.53 [1.17, 2.06] (25); inter-doom 2.02 [1.26, 3.39] (45) / n/a; control chocolate-doom 1.46 [1.29, 1.65] (226) / 1.16 [0.78, 1.70] (49).
**Same functions (DB3).** 562 inherited functions present in at least four lineages; the number of repair commits that modified each function correlates across **independent lineages**: mean pairwise Spearman **0.356** (range 0.22 to 0.50), **0.248 after removing size and call fan-in** (log size and callers in the linuxdoom graph); the shared-history control chocolate-doom with crispy-doom: 0.842.
**Same neighbours (DB4).** Inherited (cause, repair) function pairs within 2 calls in the linuxdoom graph: crispy-doom 223, dsda-doom 291, nugget-doom 319, doomretro 474, fastdoom 344, inter-doom 31, chocolate-doom 93. Pairs observed in more than one lineage (six lineages, 300 permutations replacing the repaired function by a random inherited function of the cause's 2-call neighbourhood): observed overlap **588** against a null mean of 257.3 (95th percentile 278): **2.29 times**; the shared-history control (chocolate-doom, crispy-doom): 93 against 17.1.
| hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|
| DB1 radius at least 1.3 (lower bound above 1.1) in each lineage with at least 100 pairs outside the changed set | **supported**: 1.66, 1.66, 1.50, 1.82 (lower bounds 1.36 to 1.66) |
| DB2 inherited-only radius within 25% of the all-functions radius in each lineage | **not supported**: -32% (crispy-doom, 1.13 on 89 pairs, interval 0.75 to 1.59), -7%, +2%, -18% (the inherited subsets have 79 to 199 pairs; the intervals are wide) |
| DB3 mean pairwise Spearman at least 0.20 and 0.10 after partialling size and fan-in | **supported**: 0.356 and 0.248 |
| DB4 overlap at least twice the permutation null | **supported**: 2.29 times (588 against 257) |
| DB5 shared-history pair at least twice the independent pairs | **supported** (0.842 against 0.36 for DB3; 93 observed against 17 for DB4) |
Surprise bar (partial DB3 below 0.05 and DB4 at or below the null): not met.
**Reading.** In six independent C codebases that descend from the same 1993 source, **repairs land around the same inherited functions more than their size and call fan-in explain, and the same neighbour pairs are repaired together more than chance within a neighbourhood**: two lineages that never share a commit repair `P_UseSpecialLine` and its neighbours more alike than the sizes and graph position of those functions predict. The repair radius replicates in each lineage with enough data (1.50 to 1.82). **What this cannot yet separate:** (a) structural coupling (the code around these functions is fragile) from (b) **inherited latent bugs**: all lineages carry the same original code, so the same defect can be found and repaired independently in each; both are properties of the code, not of the people, but (b) is not the "coupling" the repair radius is meant to describe; (c) **popular functions**: the DB4 null draws the repaired function uniformly from the neighbourhood, so a few functions that are repaired often in every lineage could produce the overlap alone; a popularity-matched null is needed (DB4b). Other caveats: blame labels; the "independent" lineages share borrowed code (Boom, MBF, ZDoom lineages exchanged code); name-based identity (66 to 87% of names survive); DB2's failure reflects small inherited subsets, not a tested difference.

## 8. DOOM-R1b: popularity-matched null for DB4 (hypotheses fixed 2026-11-18 before running; `tools/sensor/doom_r1b.py`)
DB4's null replaced the repaired function by a uniform draw from the cause's 2-call neighbourhood. A function repaired often in every lineage (a hotspot) could produce overlap alone. **Popularity-matched null:** the repaired function is drawn from the same neighbourhood with probability proportional to its number of repair commits in that lineage plus 0.5 (so each lineage keeps its own hotspots); 300 permutations; the same six lineages with at least 30 inherited pairs. **Trimmed:** the 20 most-repaired inherited functions (summed over lineages) are removed from both sides, the uniform null as before.
- **DB4b.** The observed overlap is at least 1.5 times the popularity-matched null mean.
- **DB4c.** After removing the 20 most-repaired inherited functions, the observed overlap is at least 1.5 times the uniform-null mean.
**Would surprise:** DB4b at or below 1.2 (then the neighbour-pair overlap of DB4 was the hotspots).

#### DOOM-R1b results (2026-11-18; `doom_r1b_output.txt`)
Observed overlap of (cause, repair) function pairs across the six lineages: **588**. Uniform null 256.8 (2.29 times). **Popularity-matched null 350.4 (95th percentile 377): 1.68 times.** With the 20 most-repaired inherited functions removed (the hotspots: the main loops and responders `D_DoomMain`, `G_Ticker`, `G_Responder`, `HU_Drawer`, `P_KillMobj`, `P_SetupLevel`, `P_SpawnMapThing`, `R_DrawPSprite`, `I_GetEvent`, `I_InitGraphics`, `M_Drawer` and similar): observed 235 against a uniform null of 99.0, **2.37 times**.
| hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|
| DB4b observed overlap at least 1.5 times the popularity-matched null | **supported**: 1.68 |
| DB4c after removing the 20 most-repaired functions, at least 1.5 times the uniform null | **supported**: 2.37 |
Surprise bar (DB4b at or below 1.2): not met.
**Reading.** Hotspots explain **about a third of the excess** (the null rises from 257 to 350 when it keeps each lineage's hotspots) but **not the effect**: the same neighbour pairs are repaired in independent lineages 1.7 times more than a popularity-matched chance, and 2.4 times without the 20 hotspots. What remains unseparated is the inherited-latent-bug explanation (same code, same defect): it is a property of the code, not of the people, but it is not structural coupling in the sense of the repair radius; separating them needs the repair diffs (do independent lineages change the same lines for the same reason?).

## 9. DOOM-R2: the same defect or the same coupling? (hypotheses fixed 2026-11-18 before running; `tools/sensor/doom_r2.py`)
DOOM-R1 and R1b show that independent lineages repair the same neighbour pairs (a change to F followed by a repair of G, both inherited, G within 2 calls of F) 1.7 to 2.4 times more than chance. Two explanations remain: **coupling** (the structure makes G fragile when F changes) and an **inherited latent defect** (G has the same bug in every lineage, found and repaired independently; the change to F is incidental). **Test: compare the repairs themselves.** For every inherited function a fix commit modified, the function's old and new text give the set of lines the repair **removed** (and the set it **added**); stripped, comments and lines of 3 characters or fewer dropped. Two repairs of the same function are the **same defect** if their removed-line sets have a Jaccard similarity of at least 0.5 (for repairs that only add lines, the added-line sets). For each (F, G) pair observed in two lineages, the similarity is the highest over the lineages' fix commits that make up the observation. Null: the repaired function replaced by a draw from the neighbourhood with probability proportional to its repair count in that lineage (the popularity-matched null of R1b), 100 permutations, the same similarity computed for the drawn function's fixes. Lineages as in R1; the positive control chocolate-doom with crispy-doom (shared commits).
- **RD1.** Fewer than half of the cross-lineage (F, G) overlaps are the same defect (similarity below 0.5 for more than half): most overlaps are not one inherited bug.
- **RD2.** Counting only overlaps that are **not** the same defect, the observed number is at least 1.5 times the popularity-matched null's number of non-same-defect overlaps (the coupling signal survives).
- **RD3.** In the positive control (chocolate-doom with crispy-doom) at least 80% of the overlaps are the same defect (the method recognises a shared repair).
**Would surprise (the effect is the inherited defect):** more than 80% of the cross-lineage overlaps are the same defect **and** RD2 at or below 1.2.

#### DOOM-R2 results (2026-11-18; `doom_r2_output.txt`), exact line comparison
Across the six independent lineages **588 overlapping (F, G) pairs: exactly 1 has two repairs of G with a removed-line Jaccard of at least 0.5** (median similarity 0.00; 100% exactly 0); the popularity-matched null has 352.4 overlaps, 332.9 of them not the same defect; **the positive control chocolate-doom with crispy-doom: 73 of 93 overlaps (78%) are the same defect**.
| hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|
| RD1 fewer than half of the overlaps are the same defect | **supported**: 0% (1 of 588) |
| RD2 overlaps that are not the same defect at least 1.5 times the popularity-matched null | **supported**: 587 against 332.9 = 1.76 |
| RD3 the positive control recognises at least 80% as the same defect | **not supported, narrowly**: 78% |
Surprise bar: not met.
**What this does and does not show.** The result looks decisive (the overlaps are not one inherited defect) but the test's **sensitivity across diverged code is unknown**: the control uses lineages that share commits and therefore identical text, whereas independent lineages have reformatted code, renamed variables and rewritten functions since 1993, so exact normalised-line equality can read 0 even for the same defect; and the null is 0 as well, so zero similarity cannot tell the two explanations apart. A comparison that survives renaming is needed (token level), calibrated against the null.

## 10. DOOM-R2b: token-level comparison of the repairs (hypotheses fixed 2026-11-18 before running; `tools/sensor/doom_r2b.py`)
**Similarity** of two repairs of one function: Jaccard of the **sets of word tokens** (identifiers, keywords, numbers; lower case) of their removed lines, or of their added lines when neither repair removes any. Same pairs as R2 (the cross-lineage overlaps of the six independent lineages), the same popularity-matched null (the repaired function drawn from the neighbourhood proportionally to its repair count in each lineage, 100 permutations, similarity computed on the drawn function's repairs), the positive control chocolate-doom with crispy-doom.
- **RD4.** The positive control's median similarity is at least 0.8 (the measure recognises a shared repair).
- **RD5.** The observed cross-lineage overlaps have a higher median similarity than the null by at least 0.05, and at least 20% of them exceed the null's 90th percentile (10% by construction): evidence that part of the overlap is the same inherited defect.
- **RD6.** If RD5 holds, the overlaps above the null's 90th percentile are no more than half of all overlaps: a majority of the overlap is not an inherited defect.
**Would surprise:** RD5 failing (observed similarity not above the null): then no part of the overlap is detectably an inherited defect and the coupling reading stands without qualification.

#### DOOM-R2b results (2026-11-18; `doom_r2b_output.txt`), token-level comparison
548 cross-lineage overlaps with repairs on both sides; 32,804 null draws (popularity-matched); 76 positive-control overlaps. **Median token similarity of the two repairs: observed 0.000, null 0.250 (90th percentile 0.778), positive control 1.000.** Share at least 0.8: observed **0%**, null 10%, control **96%**. Share of the observed overlaps above the null's 90th percentile: 0%.
| hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|
| RD4 the positive control's median similarity at least 0.8 | **supported**: 1.000 (96% at least 0.8): the token measure recognises a shared repair |
| RD5 observed median at least 0.05 above the null and 20% above its 90th percentile | **not supported**: -0.250 and 0% |
| RD6 (only if RD5 holds) | not applicable |
Surprise bar (RD5 fails): **met**: no part of the overlap is detectably an inherited defect.
**What the overlaps are (a check on the dsda-doom and nugget-doom pairs).** Of 122 comparisons of the two lineages' repairs of the same function in an overlapping (F, G) pair, 77 are removes-against-removes with token similarity near zero (for example in `G_BuildTiccmd`: one lineage changed lines about artifact keys and weapon selection, the other a `flip_levels` check), 38 have one repair removing lines and the other only adding, 7 both only add, 10 lack text. **Independent lineages repair the same neighbour pairs, but with different edits:** the overlapping G are large central functions (the main loops, ticker, responders, drawers: `G_BuildTiccmd`, `D_Display`, `M_Responder`, `HU_Responder`, `P_KillMobj`...) that every lineage edits for its own reasons. The observed similarity is **below** the null (0.000 against 0.250) because the null draws more small functions with a single repair, so the comparison is confounded by how large and busy G is (a limit of R2b, not a finding).
**Reading, with R1 and R1b.** (1) The overlap of (F, G) across independent lineages is **not the same inherited defect repaired in each lineage**: the repairs are different edits (no overlap at 0.8, 96% for a true shared repair). (2) Instead the same **central functions sit in the repair neighbourhood of many changes in every lineage**: structure (graph position and centrality) rather than people or an inherited bug; this is consistent with the radius being a property of the call structure. (3) The result is bounded by hotness: the neighbour overlap (1.7 times a popularity-matched null; 2.4 times without the 20 hotspots) shows pair-specific repetition beyond popularity, but R2b cannot say whether that residual is coupling or further busy functions. Next candidate tests: compare the **cause-side** edits (do lineages change F in the same way before repairing G?), and rerun R1b and R2b by size band and by fan-in matched null (matching G's size and fan-in, not only its repair count).

## 11. DOOM-R3: does a sibling lineage's repair history beat the local call graph? (hypotheses fixed 2026-11-18 before running; `tools/sensor/doom_r3.py`)
**Task.** For a change in a held-out lineage (a cause commit that modified inherited functions), rank the **inherited functions** of that lineage's call graph (the universe: functions whose normalised name occurs in `linuxdoom` and that exist in the nearest quarterly scan, minus the changed ones) by where the repair is expected, and see where the functions the named repair edited land (inherited, outside the changed set). Held-out lineage L in {crispy-doom, dsda-doom, nugget-doom, doomretro, fastdoom, inter-doom}; the other five are the "siblings" (chocolate-doom excluded: it shares history with crispy-doom). **No leakage in time:** a prior for a change at time t uses only repairs that happened before t in the sibling lineages. Blame-based causes as before.
**Rankers.** **L** (local, the validated ranking): same file as a changed function, then call distance (1, 2, 3 calls, either direction) in L's call graph, then function size. **H** (hotness only): the sibling lineages' number of repair commits that modified the candidate before t (sum over siblings of log(1 + count)), then size. **P** (pair prior): for the changed inherited functions F, the number of sibling lineages in which a change touching F was followed, before t, by a repair touching the candidate G (summed over F), ties by H, then size. **LH** and **LP**: reciprocal-rank fusion of L with H and with P (score = 1/(60 + rank in L) + 1/(60 + rank in the other); parameter-free, nothing tuned on the test data). Metric: the share of changes whose best-ranked repaired function is in the top 20 (and top 10, top 100) of the inherited universe; pooled and per lineage; only changes with at least one repaired inherited function outside the changed set.
- **CP1.** The pair prior alone puts a repaired function in the top 20 at least three times as often as a random ordering of the universe (the universe has about 500 to 700 functions: random is about 3%).
- **CP2.** LP raises the top-20 share over L alone by at least 3 percentage points pooled, and in at least 4 of the 6 lineages.
- **CP3.** The gain is not just popularity: LP is at least 2 points above LH (pooled).
- **CP4.** L alone keeps its strength in the inherited universe: top 20 at least 30% pooled.
**Would surprise (siblings add nothing):** LP less than 1 point above L pooled.
**Caveats fixed in advance.** Lineages differ in age and activity (dsda-doom starts in 2000, inter-doom in 2023): early changes have thin priors; the pair prior can only help for changes whose functions were changed and repaired in a sibling earlier; independence of lineages is partial (shared borrowed code); the universe is the inherited functions only (a restriction the sensor would not have).

Surprise bar (LP less than 1 point above L): **met** at the top 20.
**Reading.** **A sibling lineage's repair history is a real signal on its own** (the pair prior finds a repaired function in the top 20 for 28.5% of changes, 9 times random, and about 7 points more than popularity alone, 21.2%; at the top 100, 58.9% against 55.9%) but **it does not improve the local call-graph ranking at the top of the list**: rank fusion lowered the top 20 (42.9% against 45.3%; worse in four lineages) and raised the top 100 (79.9% against 74.8%). Where the local ranker has a strong ordering, averaging in a weaker ranker moves its best candidates down; where it runs out (positions 20 to 100) the prior fills in. **Most of what sibling history knows is which functions are busy** (H gets 21.2% alone, P 28.5%); the pair-specific part is real but small. Caveats: rank fusion is one way of combining (parameter-free, chosen in advance); six lineages of unequal size (17 to 246 changes); universe restricted to inherited functions; priors thin for early changes; partially shared code between lineages.

#### Exploratory follow-up (not pre-registered; hypotheses written before running, labelled post hoc because they were suggested by the R3 result)
- **E1.** Using the sibling prior **only as a tie-break** inside the local ordering (same file, then call distance, then the pair prior, then hotness, then size; ranker **T1**) does not lose anything at the top: its top 20 is at least the local ranker's (45.3%) and its top 100 at least 2 points above it.
- **E2.** Keeping the local ranker's top 10 and ordering the rest by the fusion of L and P (**T2**) has a top 20 within 1 point of L and a top 100 at least 3 points above L.
